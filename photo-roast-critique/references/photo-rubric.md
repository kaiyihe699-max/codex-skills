# Photography Critique Rubric

Use this when a critique needs real photographic judgment behind the jokes.

## Core Questions

1. What is the first thing the viewer sees?
2. What is the photograph trying to say?
3. Does every visible element support that idea?
4. Is the light helping the subject or fighting it?
5. Does the edit make the image clearer, or just louder?

## Diagnosis Areas

### Subject and Story

- Clear subject: viewer knows where to look within one second.
- Intent: mood, relationship, event, product, place, or visual joke is legible.
- Distractions: bright patches, messy edges, signs, limbs, random objects, duplicated shapes, or background faces pull attention away.
- Stakes: the image gives the viewer a reason to care beyond "this existed".

### Composition

- Framing: avoid amputating limbs, cutting important objects awkwardly, or leaving dead space with no purpose.
- Visual hierarchy: subject, secondary detail, and background should have a readable order.
- Lines and geometry: horizon, verticals, leading lines, frames within frames, and repeated shapes should feel intentional.
- Depth: foreground/midground/background separation often makes a flat scene feel designed.
- Crop: choose vertical, horizontal, square, or 3:4 based on subject and platform, not habit.

### Light

- Direction: front, side, back, top, mixed, or practical light should support the mood.
- Quality: soft/hard light is not good or bad by itself; judge whether it fits the face, product, or scene.
- Exposure: protect important highlights, keep skin or product detail usable, and avoid muddy shadows unless mood demands it.
- Face light: watch eye sockets, nose shadow, uneven color casts, and missing catchlights.
- Background light: bright background spots can steal the photo.

### Moment and Pose

- Gesture: hands, shoulders, chin, spine, and eye direction should feel alive rather than frozen.
- Expression: judge whether the expression matches the intended mood.
- Timing: street, event, travel, and documentary photos often live or die on the half-second.
- Relationship: if multiple people appear, their body language should create a readable connection.

### Color and Post

- White balance: skin, whites, and neutrals should not drift accidentally.
- Palette: two or three dominant color families usually read cleaner than every color shouting at once.
- Contrast: too little feels flat; too much kills detail and subtlety.
- Saturation: high saturation should feel stylized, not like the slider escaped.
- Retouching: skin, product, sky, and background edits should not look plastic, crunchy, or pasted on.

### Technical Quality

- Focus: main subject should be sharp unless intentional blur is clearly part of the idea.
- Motion: blur should read as energy, not a mistake.
- Noise and compression: judge whether it hurts the final platform size.
- Lens choice: wide-angle distortion, telephoto compression, and depth of field should fit the subject.

## Rescue Moves

- Crop tighter to remove dead space and distractions.
- Convert to black and white when color is chaotic but light and shape still work.
- Lower background brightness or saturation to restore subject priority.
- Lift subject exposure locally instead of brightening the whole image.
- Straighten horizon and verticals when the tilt does not add energy.
- Reframe a weak travel photo as detail, texture, or mood if the main subject fails.
- Delete the frame when the subject, moment, and light all fail; do not polish a photo with no reason to exist.

## Scoring

Use scores only when helpful:

- 9-10: strong publishable work with a clear point of view.
- 7-8: good image with one or two solvable weaknesses.
- 5-6: usable but under-directed; needs crop, edit, or clearer intent.
- 3-4: mostly a practice frame; one element may be salvageable.
- 1-2: no clear subject, weak light, weak moment, and little rescue value.

Prefer a memorable label after the score:

- "差一口气能出片"
- "烂片王中王候选：主体没立住"
- "废片边缘抢救成功：裁掉杂物还能看"
- "相机没错，脑子里的导演还没上班：构图主次没安排"
- "能发，但别置顶：完成度不够"
- "别修了，下次重拍更省电：主体、光线、时机都没成立"
